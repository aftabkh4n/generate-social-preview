# Style Directions

Choose a direction that fits the repository's existing voice and product evidence. Do not blend all directions into one image.

## Retro editorial

Use flat color fields, bitmap or pixel-display typography, hard-edged shadows, cropped product screenshots, code fragments, and asymmetric magazine-like composition. Keep the image graphic and mostly two-dimensional.

Positive style reference: [retro-editorial.png](../assets/style-references/retro-editorial.png)

Source: Open SaaS artwork by [Vince Canger (@vincanger)](https://github.com/vincanger), shared in [How I promoted my open source repo to 6k stars in 6 months](https://dev.to/wasp/how-i-promoted-my-open-source-repo-to-6k-stars-in-6-months-3li9).

Treat the image as style-only. Do not copy its `OPEN SAAS` branding, wording, React/NodeJS copy, screenshots, palette, or exact split layout. Extract principles such as strong type hierarchy, flat blocks, purposeful crops, and limited color.

Avoid gradients, glossy 3D objects, generic dashboards, and fake terminal decoration.

## Swiss technical

Use oversized modern typography, a strict modular grid, generous negative space, thin rules, small technical annotations, and one product-specific diagram or screenshot crop. Limit the palette to neutral paper or ink plus one signal color.

Avoid centered hero layouts, soft blobs, glassmorphism, and ornamental icons.

## Terminal collage

Build the card from one real or truthful terminal interaction, a source-owned UI fragment, and restrained annotations. Use monospaced type, hard crops, high contrast, and visible command-to-result causality.

Avoid walls of fake code, matrix rain, green-on-black nostalgia, and screenshots too small to read.

## Evidence workflow

Show a product-specific investigation or transformation as a compact evidence board: an initiating command or input, two or three truthful intermediate views, and a readable ordered workflow. Let this visual occupy roughly half the social card while the other half carries the product name and short positioning.

Positive style reference: [evidence-workflow.png](../assets/style-references/evidence-workflow.png)

Treat the image as style-only. Do not copy its `rea analyze` command, disassembly, decompiler text, hex values, labels, icons, palette, or exact panel layout. Extract the principles: visible causality, multiple evidence types, coherent grouping, and a final agent-oriented sequence.

Use real or source-grounded artifact types whenever possible. Simplify detail at thumbnail size, but keep the relationship between input, inspection, evidence, and result legible.

Avoid turning the sequence into an abstract glowing funnel, portal, neural network, or set of interchangeable floating cards.

## Diagrammatic print

Explain one transformation using a technical-manual or risograph-like diagram: input, mechanism, and output. Use flat arrows, numbered callouts, simple geometry, slight print texture, and a limited ink palette.

Avoid luminous portals, volumetric light, holograms, floating cards, and generic network graphs.

## Selection rule

Choose the direction whose medium reinforces the product:

- retro editorial for opinionated, playful, or creator-led developer products;
- Swiss technical for precise infrastructure and serious engineering tools;
- terminal collage for CLI-first products with a compelling real workflow;
- evidence workflow for analysis, observability, transformation, and agent tools with several truthful intermediate artifacts;
- diagrammatic print for products defined by a clear transformation.

If two directions are plausible, propose both in words and recommend one before generating. Do not generate multiple variants unless the user asks.
